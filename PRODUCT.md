# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Dirt bike and OHV riders planning a day in the Big Bear area (San Bernardino
National Forest, CA) — from beginners looking for mellow graded roads to experts
hunting technical rock. They arrive on phone or desktop, often before a trip,
wanting to quickly answer two questions: "is this ride worth it / within my
ability?" and "what do I legally need to ride it — a green sticker, red sticker,
or a street-legal plate?"

## Product Purpose

A single-page field guide to the best dirt bike routes around Big Bear. Each
route gives a short summary, ride details (distance, difficulty, elevation,
surface, season, highlights), the legal sticker/plate requirement, and a map —
a tile-composed thumbnail with the GPX track drawn on it, an elevation profile,
and a link out to the full interactive map. Success = a rider can scan the page,
pick a ride that matches their skill and bike, and know what they need before
they load up.

## Brand Personality

Rugged, trustworthy, trail-guide. Voice is that of an experienced local rider:
direct, practical, safety-aware, no marketing fluff. Three words: rugged,
honest, knowledgeable. The interface should feel like a well-worn backcountry
map — earthy and capable, not glossy or corporate. Legal/safety info is treated
as first-class, never buried.

## Anti-references

- **Generic SaaS template** — cream/white backgrounds, soft drop shadows,
  identical icon-cards, gradient accents. The default AI-startup look.
- **Corporate outdoor brand store (REI-style)** — stock hero photography,
  e-commerce gloss, buy buttons, lifestyle marketing.
- **Government / forest-service page** — dense, bureaucratic, table-heavy
  officialese.
- **Neon motocross / energy-drink** — aggressive neon gradients, racing
  stripes, hyper-sporty gamer styling.

## Design Principles

- **Legal clarity is the headline feature.** Sticker/plate requirements are
  always visible and color-coded; never make a rider dig for what could get them
  ticketed.
- **Honest about data.** Approximate/placeholder content (e.g. sample GPX
  tracks) is labeled as such. Never present unverified route data as surveyed
  fact; carry the verification disclaimer.
- **Scannable for a decision.** A rider should be able to compare rides at a
  glance — difficulty, distance, and requirement readable without reading prose.
- **Earthy, not glossy.** Visual weight comes from typography, contrast, and the
  map itself, not decoration or stock imagery.
- **Map is the hero of each card.** The drawn track + elevation profile carry
  the ride's character; everything else supports them.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.1 AA. Body text ≥4.5:1 contrast against the dark earthy
backgrounds (watch muted sand-on-bark combinations); large/display text ≥3:1.
Requirement badges must not rely on color alone — always pair the color with the
text label. Full keyboard operability with visible focus states on links/maps.
Honor `prefers-reduced-motion` for any hover/scale transitions. Maps and the
elevation profile carry descriptive `aria-label`s / alt text.
